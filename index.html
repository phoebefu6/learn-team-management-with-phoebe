Learn Team Management with Phoebe

How a team runs, as distinct from how to manage any one person in it. Six sessions on the design of a team: what turns a group with one manager into a team, who decides what and how a disagreement gets closed, the rituals a team keeps and what they cost in hours, safety and conflict as research actually describes them, and the remote, async and key-person risks that decide whether a team survives its best person leaving. One constructed running case, Northline, a 7-person data team, and one bench that prices its week under four designs in your browser.

The six sessions 🧭 · from a group with one manager to a team that survives its key person leaving

The running case is Northline, a constructed 7-person data team inside an invented retail company. Everyone syncs with everyone whenever something comes up, two stakeholders send competing requests to whoever answers first, nobody is sure who decides priorities, and the pipeline lives in one person's head. Ana, the lead, has six sessions to turn that into a team. Northline and everyone in it are invented for teaching, and every page says so.

🧩 Session 1 · foundations

A team is not a group of people with the same manager

Shared outcome, a boundary and work that depends on each other; communication paths n(n-1)/2; why no ideal size is proven; four team shapes; the one-page charter.

Who decides what

The team-size bench

Exact paths and a modelled week for four designs of a constructed 7-person team, the late-project anti-lever that tests Brooks's law, and the break button where the one person who knows the pipeline leaves.

Psychological safety read at source, what Google's Project Aristotle is and is not, the unsettled research on task conflict, and conflict between priorities routed through decision rights.

What remote work did to one company's collaboration network, async defaults that keep a team deciding, a shield for interrupt load, and a key-person plan checked on the bench.

What is honest here 🧾

Management advice travels with famous claims, and several of the most quoted ones are company reports, essays or misreadings. Each claim on these pages carries its status.

ClaimStatus
Communication paths n(n-1)/2, and the bench's bus factorExact: arithmetic on the declared team and its map of who knows what
Every hour figure on the bench: meetings, coordination, handoffs, focus, late-project weeksModelled by rules printed on the widget, applied to a constructed team; only the direction of each effect is claimed
"Adding people to a late project makes it later"An essay claim (Brooks, 1975, reported). On the bench's own rules it holds near the deadline and fails further out. Session 4 traces it
"Google found psychological safety is what makes teams work"A company report. Google's re:Work guide, read at source, ranks it first of five dynamics in its own 180 teams; the research behind the term is Edmondson's 1999 study, read at source. Session 5
"Task conflict is good for teams"Contested. A 2003 meta-analysis found it hurt; a 2012 one did not find a strong negative link. Session 5 teaches the disagreement
"Dunbar's number" or any ideal team sizeNot supported for work teams by any source read here. Session 1
